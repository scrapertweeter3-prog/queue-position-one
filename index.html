Queue position one is the product

A development engagement publishes its rate, its stack, its turnaround. The number that sets delivery speed sits one line lower: how many cards are in front of yours. Poket Dev runs the queue at one. Requests stack up as cards on a Trello board, Requests to Active to Approved, and one card is active at a time. The next card starts when the current one lands.

Queue arithmetic does the rest. A three-person shop carrying five clients is running five queues at once, and every client's card waits behind the other four. Assume a three-day card: the client fifth in line waits twelve days before anybody touches the work, about two and a half workweeks of silence, while the card at queue position one starts the morning it is filed. Same developers, same total hours. The wait is a scheduling choice, and it is billed either way.

The fee reads differently once the queue is priced in. At $5,995 a month with Mon-Fri delivery, the calendar holds about 22 working days, so a three-day card ships roughly seven cards a month, near $820 per delivered card. A light month with two big cards prices them at $2,998 each. The flat rate buys a queue position, not hours, so cards never get slower as more of them arrive; the constraint moves to the calendar, where it is visible, instead of hiding in somebody else's backlog.

Revisions sit inside the same card, which is where fixed bids usually spring their leaks. A typical agency rework line at $150 an hour for a ten-hour revision runs $1,500, and three revision cycles run $4,500 on top of the bid. Unlimited revisions until the card is approved price that risk at zero, and the code lands in a private repo that stays yours if the subscription ever ends.

Onboarding compresses the same way: the Trello board exists about 24 hours after signing, twelve times sooner than the fifth-in-line client above ever sees a first card start. The math to check before any development engagement is the same math: less the rate on the card, more the length of the queue behind it.

Poket Dev is the unlimited software development subscription, and the queue is one card long.
